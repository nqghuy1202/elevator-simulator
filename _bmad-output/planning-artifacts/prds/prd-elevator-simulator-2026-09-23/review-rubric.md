# PRD Quality Review — Elevator Simulator (Node.js Interview Take-Home Test)

## Overall verdict
This PRD is unusually well-earned for its stakes tier: the dispatch/state-machine FRs (FR-6 through FR-12) carry real testable consequences, the counter-metric and scope-cutting order show honest trade-off surfacing, and the Glossary/FR/UJ cross-references are consistent throughout. The main soft spots are cosmetic — an unresolved "Working title" placeholder, MVP scope citing "F1-F9" against a Features section that actually runs FR-1..FR-19, and a couple of adequate-but-thin NFR bullets. Nothing here blocks a solo candidate from building against it.

## Decision-readiness — strong
The scope-cutting order in §8 ("correctness of SCAN/LOOK business rules > clear/intentional OOP > UI polish > test coverage > Docker") is a real, ranked trade-off with an explicit tension called out against `INTERVIEW_PREP_REPORT.md`'s presentation-emphasis ranking — the PRD does not let the two documents silently contradict each other, it names the difference in framing. The counter-metric SM-C1 (§9) is a genuine "don't optimize this" guardrail, not a rhetorical one: it says a Redux+Docker shell around broken dispatch logic fails outright. §10's Open Questions are honestly closed out with pointers to where each was resolved, rather than left as unresolved-but-answered filler.

### Findings
- **low** Unresolved title placeholder (§ line 9) — "*Working title — confirm.*" sits directly under the H1 with no resolution recorded anywhere later in the doc. *Fix:* either confirm the title or drop the placeholder note.

## Substance over theater — strong
No persona theater: exactly two personas (§2.1), and the PRD is explicit that one is "the PRD's real audience of consequence" and the other is "the in-world role the FRs are written from" (line 30) — both earn their place rather than padding a persona count. No NFR boilerplate: §7's NFRs are product-specific ("simulation tick rate must feel like continuous movement... ARCHITECTURE.md proposes ~500ms/tick", "no Hall Call or Car Call is ever silently lost (FR-7 is the load-bearing guarantee here)") rather than generic "must be scalable/secure" filler. The Vision (§1) names the specific mechanism (SCAN/LOOK), the specific OOP pillars under test, and the specific stakes (45-minute presentation, legibility without narration) — it could not swap into another PRD unchanged.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is explicit and singular: demonstrate OOP design discipline and correct concurrent dispatch logic to an interview reviewer, not "build a working elevator." Every feature group traces back to that thesis — even the JD-alignment items (Redux in §4.8, Docker/Nginx in §4.9) are labeled for what they are ("Lowest-cost JD alignment item; must not consume disproportionate time," line 153) rather than pretending they're core to the simulation. Success Metrics (§9) validate the thesis directly — SM-1 measures SCAN/LOOK correctness, SM-2 measures structural OOP presence via a code-review checklist, not activity metrics. The counter-metric SM-C1 explicitly prevents the JD-alignment features from being over-invested at the thesis's expense.

## Done-ness clarity — strong
Nearly every FR carries a "Consequences (testable)" block with concrete, checkable conditions — e.g., FR-6's "Elevator moving 1→10 accepts a Hall Call ↑ at floor 5... does *not* immediately accept a Hall Call ↓ at floor 5," or FR-10's "`Elevator.tick()` contains no switch/if-chain keyed on a state name." These are falsifiable by inspection or test, not adjectives.

### Findings
- **low** FR-2's testable consequence ("Pending indicator clears only when an Elevator opens its doors at that floor for that direction") is fine, but the FR itself has no other body text beyond the header — thin relative to neighboring FRs. Not a blocker; the consequence alone carries it.
- **low** §7 "Reliability" and "Deployability" bullets lean on adjectives on their own ("a reviewer must be able to get the full stack running locally from a single documented command") but are functionally acceptance criteria already, and FR-18's testable consequence (`docker-compose up` starts both) backs Deployability concretely. No fix needed, noting only because the rubric asks to flag every soft adjective.

## Scope honesty — strong
§5 Non-Goals is doing real work, not boilerplate — eight specific exclusions each with a reason (no IoT/hardware, no auth, no persistence "state lives in server memory... a server restart resets it," no WCAG "per the test's own allowance"). §6.2 Out of Scope for MVP explicitly de-scopes Round-Robin strategy, hall-call merge optimization, max-hold-time cap, and CI — all with a stated reason, not silent omission. Two `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (FR-16, FR-17) are both indexed in §11 and both inline; roundtrip is clean. Open-items density is low (0 open questions, 2 assumptions, 0 `[NOTE FOR PM]`) — appropriate for a solo take-home with a fixed, non-negotiable deadline, not a red flag here.

## Downstream usability — adequate
This PRD is effectively standalone (feeds only the candidate's own build against `ARCHITECTURE.md`, not a separate downstream team), so per the rubric this dimension matters less. Within that lighter bar: Glossary (§3) terms are used consistently in the FRs (Hall Call, Car Call, Stop Queue, Pending Call, Dispatcher all recur with consistent capitalization). FR IDs are contiguous FR-1 through FR-19 with no gaps or duplicates. UJ-1 has a named protagonist ("Occupant on Floor 5... She's on Floor 5") and is referenced correctly by the three FRs it claims to realize (FR-6, FR-7, FR-8 — confirmed correct against §4.4).

### Findings
- **medium** MVP scope references stale IDs (§6.1, §6.2) — "F1-F9 in full, per §4 — all confirmed must-have" and "Counterbalances F8/F9 against F4/F5" (SM-C1, §9) use an `F#` numbering scheme that does not match the document's actual `FR-1..FR-19` numbering anywhere else. It's unclear whether "F1-F9" means Features 4.1-4.9 (the nine feature *groups*, which does map: Hall Call, Car Call, Door Control, Dispatch Engine, State Machine, Real-time Sync, Multi-elevator, Client State, Containerization) or a truncated/legacy FR range. If it means feature groups 4.1-4.9, it actually covers all FR-1 through FR-19, not a subset — so "in full" is consistent, but the label collision with FR-numbering is confusing on a document a candidate will be citing live in a 45-minute presentation. *Fix:* rename to "Feature groups 4.1-4.9" or otherwise disambiguate `F#` from `FR-#`.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a solo/hobby-tier take-home, correctly shaped as a lightweight capability spec with one thin UJ rather than forced into a full multi-persona journey map. The PRD explicitly names its own scope dial choice: "Scope dial: **Lighter** (solo take-home test, single occupant role) — the test's own worked example *is* the canonical journey" (§2.3, line 34) — this is a self-aware, correct calibration, not an oversight. A single UJ realizing three FRs (FR-6/7/8) is appropriate rather than under- or over-formalized. The two-persona split (occupant vs. reviewer) reflects the real stakeholder shape of a take-home test (there is a human grading it) rather than manufacturing external users that don't exist for this artifact.
